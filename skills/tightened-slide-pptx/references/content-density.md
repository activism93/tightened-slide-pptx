# Content Density and Compression

Use density to increase meaning per slide, not to shrink type or fill every gap.

## Density Tiers

| Tier | Use | Expected structure |
|---|---|---|
| D0 | Cover, divider | One title, one supporting line, optional hero visual |
| D1 | Thesis, transition, closing | One claim plus one strong proof, contrast, or action |
| D2 | Standard body | One claim, two to four evidence modules, one visual/data structure |
| D3 | Technical body | One claim, three to five tightly related evidence modules, one primary technical diagram or comparison |

Do not use D0 or D1 repeatedly to inflate the page count. Most technical body slides should be D2 or D3.

## Compression Pass

1. Inventory required facts, equations, diagrams, examples, trade-offs, and citations.
2. Group them by the audience question they answer.
3. Give each group one takeaway title and one primary visual structure.
4. Merge adjacent groups when they share the same question, visual basis, and conclusion.
5. Keep groups separate when they require different mental models, independent diagrams, or clearly different decisions.
6. Remove repeated definitions and move long bibliographic detail to speaker notes or a final reference slide.

Merge two candidate slides when both have fewer than two useful evidence modules and the combined slide still fits at 16 pt body text or larger. Do not merge merely to hit an arbitrary slide count.

## Body-Slide Minimum

A standard body slide should normally contain:

- one takeaway title;
- one short lead sentence or framing question;
- two to four evidence modules such as a mechanism, number, equation, comparison, implication, or constraint;
- one native diagram, chart, table, or bounded visual asset;
- one explicit implication or transition when it advances the story.

A title plus one sentence and large unused space is acceptable only for a deliberate D1 statement slide.

## Planning Ledger

Record this before authoring:

```text
page | question | takeaway title | density | evidence modules | layout | visual | merge rationale
```

Flag any D2/D3 slide with fewer than two evidence modules. Flag any adjacent pair that repeats a definition, equation, or conclusion.

## Visual Density

- Use one primary composition, not a grid of decorative cards.
- Let a technical diagram carry several related facts through native labels and callouts.
- Keep at least one intentional quiet zone, but do not reserve 30% empty space when it makes a technical slide feel unfinished.
- Prefer two meaningful regions over four shallow panels.
- Shorten copy before reducing body text below 16 pt.
